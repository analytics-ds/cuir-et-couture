Plenty of brands write "stretch leather" on a label. Very few actually work the composite, because it is neither cut nor sewn like an ordinary hide, and a production line used to regular leather gets it wrong.

Here is the state of play, segment by segment, with prices recorded directly on the houses' own websites on 21 September 2026.

## Upstream: the manufacturer, not the brand

The real reference in stretch leather is not a fashion house, it is a manufacturer. In **Graulhet**, in the Tarn, **Cuirs du Futur** splits lambskin down to 0.2 or 0.3 mm and bonds it onto cotton canvas made in France. The company holds the Entreprise du Patrimoine Vivant label, makes 60 percent of its business from exports, and counts **Dior** and **Chanel** among its clients.

In other words, some of the houses below buy the same material. What separates them is what they do with it: the cut, the assembly, where it is made and the service around it.

## The couture segment: Jitrois

The Paris house [Jitrois](https://jitrois.com/) is the one that introduced stretch leather in collection, in 1995, and the only one of the three to claim a **couture** position rather than ready-to-wear.

Its composite pairs lambskin with a backing at **97 percent cotton and 3 percent elastane**. Pieces are **handmade in Paris**, and the house offers made to measure, including online.

Prices follow: across the trousers collection, from **2,490 euros** for a flared stretch lambskin suede model to **4,490 euros** for wide-leg trousers in patinated leather. This is the top of the market, and the only place where the process is treated as a couture subject, with the finishing that goes with it.

## The ready-to-wear segment: Stouls

[Stouls](https://stouls.paris/) built its brand on a single promise, an entire wardrobe in stretch leather: trousers, skirts, dresses, jumpsuits, coats.

Its material is **tanned in France**, the making is done **in Portugal**, in an atelier the house presents as aligned with its standards. Its distinctive feature is one property of the composite it has pushed further than the others: its pieces are **machine washable**.

Prices recorded across the trousers collection run from **967 euros** for the Le Jo legging to **1,792 euros** for Le Valadon. One notch below the couture segment, for a more everyday garment.

## The single-product segment: SPRWMN

In the United States, [SPRWMN](https://www.sprwmn.com/) took the opposite angle: one garment, the **leather legging**, in various lengths and colours.

The brand states **100 percent French plonge lambskin**, lined with cotton-Lycra. The crop flare legging is listed at **1,175 dollars**. That is what makes the case interesting: the material crosses the Atlantic, but it comes from the same French tanneries.

## How to read those price gaps

A fourfold gap between a legging and a couture trouser is not explained by the hide, which may come from the same manufacturer. It is explained by three items:

- **Where it is made.** An hour in a Paris atelier does not cost the same as an hour in a Portuguese one, and a handmade piece takes dozens of hours.
- **How complex the cut is.** A legging has few panels, a wide-leg couture trouser has many, each of which must be cut along the right stretch direction.
- **The service.** Made to measure, alterations and repairs are paid for in the initial price.

Below 500 euros, be careful: you leave the leather composite for coated leather or elastane-backed imitation, which stretches but does not age the same way. The three verification checks are detailed in [our definition of stretch leather](/en/stretch-leather/stretch-leather-definition/).

## What this landscape says about the market

Stretch leather remains a specialist market. Thirty years after its introduction, it has not been absorbed by the large ready-to-wear houses, which use it occasionally without making it a signature, and it has not moved down market.

The reason is simple: the material is easy to buy and hard to assemble. It is the assembly, not the hide, that separates a piece lasting ten years from one that blisters within a season.
